What a good cost per lead actually looks like in India in 2026

Real ranges from live accounts across D2C, clinics, real estate and SaaS — and the three things that explain almost every number that lands three times higher than the band.

BM Brand Marketian ·Aug 2026 ·4 min read
Cost per lead, by category

Key takeaways

  • "Good" CPL is meaningless without the category, the offer and the lead definition attached to it.
  • Across our live Indian accounts, most healthy campaigns sit inside a fairly narrow band per industry — the table below.
  • When a CPL is 2–3× the band, it is almost never the bid. It is the offer, the audience match, or a leaky form.
  • Chase cost per qualified lead, not cost per form fill. They are different numbers and only one pays salaries.

Every founder who runs ads eventually asks the same question: "Is my cost per lead good?" It feels like it should have a clean answer, like a cricket score. It doesn't — because a ₹120 lead for a ₹499 skincare sample and a ₹120 lead for a ₹40-lakh apartment are not the same event. But there is a useful band for each category, and knowing yours tells you in thirty seconds whether to celebrate, tune, or rebuild.

Here is roughly where healthy campaigns land for us in 2026, on Meta and Google, for Indian audiences. Treat these as the middle of the road, not a promise — your number moves with city tier, season and how tight your targeting is.

If you are inside your band, your job is not to lower the number — it is to hold it while you scale spend. If you are well above it, the number is a symptom. Here is what it is usually a symptom of.

1. The offer is doing the heavy lifting — or the dragging

Nine times out of ten, a CPL three times the band is an offer problem wearing a targeting costume. "Book a consultation" is a weak ask; "Book a free 15-minute skin analysis, this week only" is a strong one. Same audience, same budget, half the CPL. Before you touch the bid, look at what you are actually asking the click to do and what they get for it.

A high CPL is rarely a bidding problem. It's usually the offer, the match, or a leaky form.

2. Audience–offer match, not audience size

Broad targeting is cheap to reach and expensive to convert if the creative doesn't pre-qualify. The fix is not a smaller audience — it is a creative that says who this is for in the first three seconds, so the wrong people scroll past and don't cost you a click. Let the creative do the filtering.

3. The form is quietly leaking

We regularly find 30–50% of the CPL problem sitting after the click: a form that asks for eight fields, a landing page that takes four seconds to load on 4G, an instant-form with no qualifying question so half the leads are mistakes. Cutting a form from seven fields to three, or adding one qualifying question, moves cost per qualified lead more than any bid change.

What we'd actually do

Pull last 30 days by campaign. Mark every campaign inside its band as "hold & scale", everything 1.5×+ over as "diagnose". For the diagnose pile, check in order: offer strength, first-3-second creative, form length, page speed. Fix in that order and re-measure after 7 days — not 2.

The number that actually matters

Cost per lead is a vanity metric until you divide your leads into qualified and junk. A ₹90 CPL where one in ten is real is worse than a ₹220 CPL where one in two closes. Wire your CRM so sales can tag lead quality, feed that back to the ad platform, and optimise toward the qualified event. That single change quietly re-prices your whole account.
