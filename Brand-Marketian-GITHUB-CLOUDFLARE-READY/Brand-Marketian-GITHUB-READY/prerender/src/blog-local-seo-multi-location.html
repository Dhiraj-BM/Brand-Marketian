Local SEO for multi-location businesses

One Google Business Profile per location, done properly, beats any amount of blog content. Here's the unglamorous checklist that actually moves you into the local pack.

BM Brand Marketian ·Jun 2026 ·6 min read

Key takeaways

  • For "near me" demand, the map pack matters far more than your blog. Fix the profiles first.
  • One fully-completed, verified Google Business Profile per location — never one shared listing.
  • Reviews and consistent NAP (name, address, phone) are the two biggest levers most brands ignore.
  • Give each location its own landing page. A single "our stores" page can't rank for every city.

If you run five clinics, three outlets or a dozen franchise units, you don't have an SEO problem — you have a local SEO problem, and it's a completely different sport. Nobody searching "dermatologist near me" scrolls to a blog. They tap one of the three businesses in the map pack. So the whole game is getting each of your locations into that pack, in its own neighbourhood. Blog content is a nice-to-have; the profiles are the business.

One profile per location, fully filled

Each physical location needs its own verified Google Business Profile — same brand, distinct listing, real local address and phone. Then actually complete it: correct category, hours, services, and fresh photos of that location. A half-filled profile ranks below a competitor's complete one, every time. This is the single highest-ROI hour you'll spend all quarter.

Nobody searching "near me" reads your blog. They tap the map. Win the map.

NAP consistency — boring, decisive

Your Name, Address and Phone must match exactly everywhere they appear — Google profile, website, Justdial, Sulekha, Facebook, directories. Google reads inconsistency as uncertainty, and uncertainty costs rankings. Pick one canonical format for each location and enforce it across every listing. It's tedious. It works.

Reviews are the ranking factor you control

Volume, recency and rating of reviews on each profile move local rankings and, more importantly, the click. A location with 120 recent 4.6-star reviews beats one with 15 old ones before the searcher has read a word. Build a simple habit: every happy customer gets a direct review link (per location) by WhatsApp or SMS the same day. Reply to every review, good or bad — Google notices, and so do customers.

Give every location a real page

A single "locations" page can't rank for "salon in Indiranagar" and "salon in HSR". Build a dedicated, indexable landing page per location — local address, embedded map, that branch's services, staff, photos, reviews and localised copy. Link them from a clean store-locator. This is what lets each branch rank in its own area instead of fighting the others for one URL.

What we'd actually do

Audit and claim every profile, standardise NAP across all directories, ship a landing page per location, and set up a same-day review-request flow. Only then layer on locally-relevant content. In that order, you'll see map-pack movement in weeks — not the months a blog-first approach takes.

The honest summary

Local SEO rewards operational discipline more than clever content. Complete profiles, consistent details, steady reviews and a page per location will out-perform a beautiful blog nobody local is searching for. Do the boring things properly and the "near me" traffic is yours.
